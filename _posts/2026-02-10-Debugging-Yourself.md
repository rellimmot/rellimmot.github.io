I wrote the kind of letter that you don't intend to send. Frustration was keeping me awake, so I needed to put words down so I could go back to sleep. I put the letter in my desk and slept on it. What came out was less about the frustration and more about what I learned when the system that was failing wasn't the one on my bench. I feel like it's worth cleaning up and sharing as a vulnerable part of my career journey.

<!--more-->

## The Bug

Any engineer can have a moment where they stop troubleshooting the hardware and start troubleshooting themselves. Mine came in 2025.

I had a period where I was noticeably more difficult to work with. I was combative, contradictory, stubborn about my ideas and my ways, and I wasn't accepting feedback well — mostly with my manager, sometimes with my colleagues. My manager — freshly promoted, learning his own role under pressure — found my upper limit, and one day I lost my temper and spilled it in a private text message with them. That was the red flag that got attention. But the real pattern was quieter than a blowup.

We've since found the kind of healing through this difficulty that the majority of married couples should take therapy notes from. I've treated that major red flag occasion as a call for immediate serious action. The engineering spirit in me saw this as a challenge to solve, and I solved it.

## The Root Cause

My psychologist and I identified major pharmacological issues which in retrospect were greatly exacerbating irritability. After making those adjustments to my ADHD treatment I have not felt those ways since. My manager attests we no longer struggle in the same way that we were.

That's the thing about ADHD medication — when it's wrong, it doesn't just affect focus. It affects temperament, patience, the distance between stimulus and reaction. The bug wasn't in my character. It was pharmacological, and it had been compounding silently for months. Root cause found. Fix applied.

## The Double Edge

ADHD is [disproportionately prevalent among entrepreneurs and CEOs](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/865321) and there's a reason for that. The same wiring that makes focus unreliable on demand also produces hyperfocus — the ability to tunnel into the hardest problem in the room and not come up for air until it's solved. It's what makes me a dynamic generalist engineer. It's the thing I've put the most effort into shifting from a disability into a differentiator. David Neeleman, founder of JetBlue, [said it plainly](https://www.additudemag.com/adhd-entrepreneur-stories-jetblue-kinkos-jupitermedia/): with all the bad things that come with ADHD, there also come creativity and the ability to take risks — and if he could take a magic pill to get rid of it, he wouldn't. I understand that completely. But sometimes the same trait that makes you brilliant at the bench makes you difficult at the table. The medication adjustment wasn't about dulling the edge. It was about keeping the blade from cutting me too.

## The Performance

Those behavioral factors stood in contrast to stellar developmental performance under profound deadline pressure, a laser focused conviction in solving the most difficult engineering problems the team faces, an experienced eye for catching and solving defects in development before they reach the factory, and a strong practice of communication and collaboration with my colleagues across the department. I was still approachable, helpful, and collaborative.

I maintain a forward-looking vision and way of work, I do not struggle with loosely defined creative abstractions, I create well documented products that solve root causes, I foster standards, and I mentor my colleagues.

But technical excellence without interpersonal reliability is an incomplete system. I was delivering the work while undermining the trust. That's the hardest kind of bug to see in yourself — the one where all your tests pass but your system is still failing.

## Steadfast

The promotion to Senior Manufacturing Test Engineer didn't come this yearly cycle — a role I had been performing for over two years. I won't pretend that didn't sting. But I do so with no resent. I love my team, my manager, my upper management, and the dynamic engineering challenges we face. The fix I made wasn't for the title — it was for me, and for the people who have to work with me every day.

Regardless, I am steadfast.
