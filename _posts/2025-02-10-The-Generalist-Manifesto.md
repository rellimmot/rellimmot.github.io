I wrote a cover letter applying for a position as a Principal Manufacturing Test Engineer within my company (that I was not yet qualified for, it was ambitious but it was more about making a statement that says *grow me into your perfect Principal Engineer*.) What came out was less about the job and more about what engineering means to me. What follows is that letter, lightly adapted.

![Self-Operating Napkin - Artist: Rube Goldberg](/assets/images/posts/generalist-manifesto/SelfOperatingNapkin.jpg)

<!--more-->

## Pidgeon Holes Are for the Birds

This Principal Engineer position is an eventuality for me. If not today, then someday. I will never be able to turn off the part of my brain which wants to grow old at the bench and be an Engineer for engineering's sake. I do not willingly have a strong ambition to be a manager of people but when I do it I'm good at it. I will, however, manage people's projects and the direction of development, but the romance isn't in it for me managing people—rather it's in solving the most challenging problems. I do people management now in my Air Force Reserves job as a Master Sergeant already and I actually like it a lot in those monthly doses and despite the kind words of my troops giving me a feeling of paternalistic warmth that keeps me reenlisting for more it's not my calling. Nay.

Make me the neurotic genius who juggles all the disciplines who's a little good at everything and very good at a lot of things but isn't the best at any one thing. I humbly do not know what I do not know but I always enlist the expertise of those who do. I have an open-sourced ethos of collaboration, an awesome respect for those who have honed their craft, and a spirit of comradery unafraid to solicit aid—and I believe in my core that is the kind of anti-siloing mindset that is changing Manufacturing for the best as it has been championed in practice by my team, my manager, and myself. Pidgeon holes are for the birds, overspecialty is for the overcautious, get good at everything and don't be afraid of anything.

Should this prospective position not go to me then I empower *you* to inform the hire that I am their replacement, their underboss, and their student—and that I should consult with them about what I know so we can make my ideas happen, or so I may be corrected and redirect my energy as it aligns with their vision. If I am raw talent then mold me so that I may do the same.

## Hardened but Not Broken

The experience I bring is a technical leader hardened but not broken by my years on the Air Force flightline. Then leading small teams of PV solar installers — putting up a half gigawatt of PV under my leadership. Then an internship at the USDA for a position that did not yet exist, in an instrumentation lab that was not yet built, whose legacy of ideas I created and then passed to my replacement — and which today is still blooming my research papers and advancements in applied instrumentation in cotton production. And tenure with a Navy Special Projects Hardware Engineering Lab with as many stories as technical accomplishments and enough days at sea to make me believe I'm a sailor lost at land.

While I have not yet been recognized with the title I have been a self-starting and driven employee who can be given a full-stack project and effectively complete it by myself or through driving a small team.

During my tenure I have seen multiple large projects to completion in an independent and in a team capacity. My role for our next-generation product's manufacturing test — code development, test hardware revision, NPI, and validation — was critical for the company successfully meeting development targets. I have saved a legacy production line from halts more times than we have business needing to, such as how in my first month of employment I solved a manufacturing stoppage for a brownout sensor problem that had already made its rounds stumping the entire Product Engineering Division for months.

In an international factory transition I was instrumental in tooling and logistics, documentation of progress, and communication of needs, and I was fervent in my test hardware refurbishment. I channeled all my talent of years as a fabrication hobbyist and aircraft radar systems technician and performed a highly resourceful in-situ refurbishment of all the Test Heads resulting in an across-all-products boost in production. The Test Heads and Test Stations as they had arrived overseas with years of battle hardening and low-quality repair now perform well enough to have the team reconsidering why we should work so hard to retire it. I see that a lot of our factory's productivity suffers not as a matter of equipment quality but as a matter of equipment maintenance. We actually have wonderful test equipment, pending light refurbishment and much needed code refactoring.

I continue to compulsively troubleshoot, solve, and identify deep technical mysteries and faults in the products we sell to our customers and in our manufacturing test equipment. I want to cure all the badly crafted tests we are throwing at our products. Defect slippage dies when the tests are honest.

## This Trove of Opportunities

I will execute on the ambitions lit thus far by this trove of opportunities. I will see to it that this same fresh energy goes into the ongoing strategic refactoring of our Manufacturing Test Systems. I do not have the bandwidth to do it all alone, so I will wholly empower my team to work with me to execute on the vision. In being a technical leader, I will engage my colleagues in a culture of quality.

In the meantime, I am continuing to develop a revamp of our applied QMS strategy where I can squeeze in the time—writing the guidelines, the training, and the rollout on a Manufacturing Test Applied QMS and Software Deployment Scheme whereby we will have a revised software versioning and release process with improved tracking, visibility, and ease while leveraging existing tools.

I will continue to develop refactored code which untwists a decade's deep puzzle of variables and spaghetti code, refactors or deprecates redundant and unreliable tests, and drastically improves reporting metrics.

Our team and I will deliver on a new programming station as I take over its PCB design. I will also develop a next generation of conducted measurement Test Heads on improved Test Stations. I advocate for PXI chassis upgrades, refreshed capabilities with new PXI cards, and contracted fabrication of new Test Station wire harnesses for their repair. I advocate for right-sized refurbishment and expansion of our PXI testing architecture as it's proven entirely to be a reliable hardware system despite the unreliable code we throw at it. I advocate for the broad simplification of our Test Heads, wherein rather than redundancy we double quantity to increase test throughput.

I will hold our deliverables to a high standard of quality, because I will also set the standard of what good work looks like. I will aid my peers in learning to perform better in areas they want to improve in so that I may more effectively delegate development to them.

## Make Cool Things

Honestly, in considering me for this position I want you to ask my manager if I make him a better manager and ask him to quantify how. Ask him about my vision, my ideas and my unrelenting creativity. I want you to ask my colleagues how big I dream, and if I have the ideas to back the ambitions. Ask my colleagues if I can learn fast and adapt well. Ask my teammates if I inspire them and teach them, and if they learn skills from me.

The one thing I lack which holds me back is a lack of hands and a lack of time, so empowering me to empower others can make the dream a reality. I will also ensure that consistent with my knack for technical writing, our processes are documented and cataloged to the extent that if I disappear my hat will be the right size for my successor to wear.

I will make us make cool things, and I will make my most earnest attempt in having a good time doing it.

I made a point to write this before reading the job ad, since my manager just told me about it this afternoon. I'm glad I did that exercise because this job reads like a technical distillation of this very statement. Sometimes you know what you are before anyone tells you.
